# Classical comparison with all event activity charged

The matched classical benchmark is a finite-state, time-homogeneous Markov clock whose complete device has total physical event intensity at most $`2g`$ in every state. This includes its reset circuitry and auxiliary states. Its calibrated signed count has time-estimation diffusion at least $`1/(2g)`$, independently of its dimension, topology, thermal affinities or hidden transitions.

The argument below is an elementary uniformization bound. It is a benchmark used by this construction, not a new classical uncertainty relation. More general and tighter asymptotic time-estimation limits are available in [Prech et al., *Optimal Time Estimation and the Clock Uncertainty Relation for Stochastic Processes*, Physical Review X 15, 031068 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/rpls-mp8z).

## 1. Device and readout convention

Let $`i`$ be the complete internal state. A physical event channel $`c`$ occurs at rate $`w_c(i)`$, chooses a new state from a fixed kernel $`p_c(\cdot\mid i)`$, and contributes a fixed bounded signed mark $`y_c(i,i')`$ to the output count. The capture convention uses marks $`+1,-1,0`$. Require

```math
\lambda(i):=\sum_cw_c(i)\leq\lambda_*:=2g
\qquad\text{for every state }i.
```

The sum includes unobserved transitions, reverse transitions, hot and cold reset events, and counted self-events. An event that returns to the same internal state still consumes its physical rate allowance. In particular, a reset opportunity cannot be removed from this account merely because its sampled destination equals its starting state. All autonomous controller states that can affect the clock belong to the device.

Let $`N(t)`$ be the sum of the signed output marks, and suppose its stationary mean current is $`j>0`$. The estimator is $`\widehat t=N(t)/j`$, using the supplied current of this actual device. Define

```math
D_{\rm time}=
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}[N(t)/j]}t.
```

As in the quantum application, estimating an unknown calibration parameter is a separate task. The output supplies event marks or their accumulated count, without externally supplied timestamps.

## 2. Uniformization proof

Run an independent Poisson process of opportunities at rate $`\lambda_*`$. At an opportunity in state $`i`$, choose physical channel $`c`$ with probability $`w_c(i)/\lambda_*`$, then draw its destination from $`p_c(\cdot\mid i)`$. With remaining probability $`1-\lambda(i)/\lambda_*`$, make a dummy self-transition with mark zero. The dummy event is only a mathematical device; it is not additional physical hardware or a resource supplied to the decoder.

This reproduces the original continuous-time marked process. The Poisson waiting times are independent of the embedded discrete marked chain. Its state transition kernel is $`P=I+G/\lambda_*`$, where $`G`$ is the ordinary state generator, with physical self-events retained in the mark law. A stationary distribution of $`G`$ is also stationary for $`P`$.

For stationary initialization, every embedded mark $`Y_r`$ therefore has mean $`j/\lambda_*`$. If $`K(t)\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_*t)`$ is the opportunity count, then

```math
N(t)=\sum_{r=1}^{K(t)}Y_r,\qquad
\mathbb E[N(t)\mid K(t)=k]=\frac{kj}{\lambda_*}.
```

No independence of successive marks is assumed. They may carry any correlations generated by the complete finite classical device. Conditional variance gives the finite-time bound

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Var}N(t)
&=\mathbb E[\mathrm{Var}(N(t)\mid K(t))]
+\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb E[N(t)\mid K(t)])\\
&\geq\frac{j^2}{\lambda_*^2}\mathrm{Var}K(t)
=\frac{j^2t}{\lambda_*}.
\end{aligned}
```

Hence

```math
\boxed{D_{\rm time}\geq\frac1{\lambda_*}=\frac1{2g}.}
```

For a fixed finite irreducible clock started in a specified nonstationary state, the same asymptotic result holds. The expected accumulated embedded reward is $`kj/\lambda_*+b_k`$ with $`b_k`$ uniformly bounded; this follows by summing the finite-chain transient terms, including bounded periodic terms. Replacing the exact conditional mean above by this expression changes its variance by at most $`O(\sqrt t)+O(1)`$. Division by $`t`$ gives the same lower bound. Stationarity therefore simplifies the proof without providing a long-time performance advantage unavailable from a fixed initial state.

The bound is attainable. A directed classical cycle with $`k`$ states, all event rates $`2g`$, and one positive tick per completed circuit has independent Erlang intervals. It has

```math
j=\frac{2g}{k},\qquad
F=\frac1k,\qquad
\mathcal P=k,\qquad
D_{\rm time}=\frac1{2g}.
```

The case $`k=1`$ is a marked Poisson self-event, which is included in the resource convention above.

## 3. Comparison with the compiled quantum family

The [quasiideal application](quasiideal_application.md) uses exactly the same upper bound $`2gI`$ on the sum of all its jump effects. Its two auxiliary levels, thermal reverse events, capture channels and release mechanism are all included. For each fixed $`0<\eta<1`$ and sufficiently large $`d`$, it gives

```math
j_d=\Theta_\eta(g/d),\qquad
\mathcal P_d\geq c_\eta d^{2-\eta},\qquad
gD_{{\rm time},d}\leq C_\eta d^{-1+\eta}\longrightarrow0.
```

Every classical clock in the stated complete-device class has $`gD_{\rm time}\geq1/2`$. No restriction on its number of auxiliary states, thermal bias, or network topology is needed for this benchmark. The comparison is therefore not based on charging classical reset delays while omitting the quantum reset.

At a matched positive current $`j=j_d`$, the same inequality can be expressed as

```math
F_{\rm C}=jD_{{\rm time},{\rm C}}\geq\frac{j_d}{2g},
\qquad
\mathcal P_{\rm C}=F_{\rm C}^{-1}\leq\frac{2g}{j_d}
=O_\eta(d).
```

Matching the current matters when comparing precision alone. The direct diffusion comparison already charges the mean output period, so a slower classical output cannot evade it.

This result concerns finite-state Markov event dynamics with the full activity constraint. A deterministic continuous phase, an uncharged external time reference, or an additional autonomous device whose activity is omitted changes the comparison class. The result also does not assign zero energetic cost to a counter or detector: it isolates the finite-rate thermal reset contribution and compares the calibrated counting observables of the two fully specified generators.
